# Name candidates — Puzzle Ladder (working title)

Product: a chess.com-puzzle-style, gamified course that teaches non-technical adults
to stop asking AI for answers and start asking for the **tools and checks** that make
the right-answer steps exact — and to prove it. Thesis: *determinism comes from the
tools you force the AI through, not the words you give it. Don't let it guess the part
that has a right answer.*

Ratings are self-assessed 1–5: **Mem** = memorability, **Clr** = clarity for
non-technical adults, **Fit** = fit with the thesis. **TM** flags a trademark-collision
risk I already know of (not web-checked — a separate agent verifies).

Hard constraint honored throughout: no name implies the AI "becomes deterministic" or
"can't be wrong." The determinism lives in the *tool/check*, not the AI. Names lean on
the learner's move (ask for the tool, prove it) rather than on taming the model.

## Direction A — chess / puzzle / ladder metaphors

The learned-skill, one-move-at-a-time, climb-the-rungs framing. Matches the chess.com
puzzle loop and the Guesser→Architect rank arc.

| Name | Rationale | Tagline | Mem | Clr | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Puzzle Ladder** (incumbent) | Describes the format literally: puzzles you climb. Safe, a little generic. | "Climb from guessing to proof, one puzzle at a time." | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| **The Next Move** | Chess framing + the product's actual verb: every level you add the next *move* to your ask. "Moves" is already the collectible. | "Every ask has a next move." | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| **Mate in One** | Chess puzzle term for a forced win — the move that ends the guessing. Playful, memorable; "mate" may read oddly to pure non-chess adults. | "One move turns a guess into a sure thing." | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| **Rung** | A single step of the ladder; short, ownable, app-store friendly. Abstract on its own. | "One rung at a time, from guess to proof." | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| **Open File** | Chess term (an open file is a line of attack) that puns on *opening the file* — the move that stops the AI inventing data (M5, pin the source). | **Endgame** | The part of chess where precise technique beats improvisation — exactly the thesis. Strong word, heavy TM/pop-culture overload. **TM:** Marvel film; countless products. | "Where guessing ends and technique wins." | 4 | 4 | 4 |

## Direction D — tools-not-answers / "ask for the tool"

The behavior change at the center of the course: ask for the thing that produces the
answer, not the answer.

| Name | Rationale | Tagline | Mem | Clr | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ask for the Tool** | The single most important move (M2), stated as the name. Teaches the behavior just by being read; a non-technical adult knows what to do differently. Long-ish but a clean imperative. | "Don't ask for the answer. Ask for the tool." | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| **Hand It the Tools** | Reframes the learner as the one in charge, handing the AI tools. Active, warm; slightly ambiguous about *who* uses the tools. | "You hand it the tools. It does the work." | 3 | 4 | 4 |
| **Toolwright** | A maker of tools (like wheelwright) — the Architect rank distilled. Distinctive, ownable, a touch archaic for non-technical adults. | "Make the AI build the tool, not fake the answer." | 3 | 2 | 4 |
| **Make It Work** | Double meaning: make it *run the tool* (M3) and make the whole thing *work*. Punchy, Carl-voice. Idiom is generic; thesis carried by tagline. | "Make it write the formula — then make it run it." | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| **Show Me the Work** | Move M4 verbatim; a phrase every adult heard in school, so clarity is instant and the ask is obvious. Narrower than the full thesis (one move of several). | "Never accept the answer. Make it show the work." | 5 | 5 | 4 |
| **The Tool, Not the Answer** | Spells out the swap exactly. Great for a subtitle/positioning line; a bit long and compound as the primary mark. | "The tool, not the answer." | 3 | 5 | 5 |

## Direction E — checks / proof ("seen it fail" / green that lied)

The keystone world: a check only counts once it's been seen going red; a green that
can't fail is a lie.

| Name | Rationale | Tagline | Mem | Clr | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Seen It Fail** | The product's own law (P2): a check you trust is one you've watched go red on a bad example. Memorable, slightly cryptic until explained; superb once it lands. | "Trust the check you've seen fail." | 4 | 3 | 5 |
| **Prove It** | The imperative behind every pass criterion. Short, confident, universally understood, Carl-voice dry. Common phrase → ownership/TM risk. **TM:** apps and a snack brand use "Prove It." | "Don't believe the green. Prove it." | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| **Make It Fail** | The counterintuitive keystone move: a good check must be able to fail. Grabby, teaches the non-obvious idea in three words; could read negative out of context. | "A check that can't fail isn't a check." | 4 | 3 | 5 |
| **The False Green** | Names the exact villain of World 4 — the checker that passed because it couldn't fail. Vivid and specific; needs a beat to explain, so better as a chapter/world name than the whole product. | "The green that lied — and how to catch it." | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| **Red First** | A check should go red on the bad case before you ever trust its green (prove-it-can-fail first). Crisp, techy-adjacent; the logic isn't self-evident to a non-technical adult until taught. | "See it go red first. Then trust the green." | 3 | 2 | 4 |
| **Catch the Miss** | Benefit of a real check for a non-technical adult: the thing that quietly broke gets caught. Plain, friendly, outcome-forward. | "4 of 8 slipped through. A real check catches them." | 3 | 4 | 4 |

## Top 8 (my recommendation)

Chosen for the triangle of memorability + non-technical clarity + thesis fit, with a bias
toward names that *teach the behavior just by being read* and don't imply a tamed/
infallible AI.

1. **Ask for the Tool** — the one behavior change, stated as the name. Teaches M2 on sight; perfect thesis fit; a clean imperative. (5/5/5)
2. **Don't Let It Guess** — the learner line verbatim; a non-technical adult gets the whole product in four words. Long for a logo but unbeatable on clarity. (5/5/5)
3. **Prove It** — short, confident, universal; carries the keystone (checks/proof). Watch the TM crowding. (5/5/5, TM risk)
4. **Who Did the Math?** — the thesis-carrying label turned into a question that reframes every AI answer; sticky and provocative. (5/5/5)
5. **The Next Move** — chess + the product's actual verb ("moves" are the collectible); ties the metaphor to the mechanic. (4/4/4)
6. **Show Me the Work** — move M4 verbatim; schoolroom-familiar so clarity is instant and the ask is obvious. Slightly narrower than the full thesis. (5/5/4)
7. **Seen It Fail** — the course's own law as a name; the most *distinctive* option and a perfect fit, costs one sentence to explain. (4/3/5)
8. **Exactly** — one warm word for the outcome, scales to a brand voice; tagline must keep the exactness attached to the tool, not the AI. (4/4/4)

### Notes for the next step
- **Strongest safe pick:** *Ask for the Tool* — teaches the behavior, no TM baggage I know of, no "infallible AI" implication.
- **Highest ceiling, needs TM check:** *Prove It* (crowded), *Endgame*/*Checkmate* (overloaded + mild "unbeatable AI" tension — flagged).
- **Keep as world/chapter names even if not the product name:** *The False Green* (W4), *Make It Fail* (W4), *Mate in One*, *The Workshop*.
- Every top-8 tagline keeps determinism on the tool/check, never on the model — e.g. "a tool did the math," "a tool checked it," "a check it can't fake."
